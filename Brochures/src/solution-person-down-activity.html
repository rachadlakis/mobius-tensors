Person Down & Activity

Know when someone falls, and what everyone was doing.

The problem

A worker collapses in an empty aisle, a guest slips on the stairs at night. Cameras see it, but nobody is watching that screen, and finding it later means hours of scrubbing.

What it does

Histor reads the body of every person it tracks: standing, sitting, crouching or lying, hands up or down, and the colour of their top and trousers. It alerts you when someone stays down, and remembers the rest so you can ask about it later.

How it works

  1. 1
    TrackEach person gets an ID and is followed.
  2. 2
    Read the body17 points: head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles.
  3. 3
    RememberPosture, hands and colours are stored whenever they change.
  4. 4
    Alert or answerSomeone down for 10 s is an alert; any question later gets an exact answer.

What you get

  • Person downAlert when someone stays lying for 10 seconds, with a snapshot.
  • Hands upBoth hands held above the head for 3 seconds, at a till or a desk.
  • Posture historySeconds standing, sitting or lying, per person and per visit.
  • What they woreMain colour of the top and of the trousers, from 11 colour names.
  • Ask in plain words"Did anyone fall in the warehouse last night?" "A red top and blue jeans?"
  • Stays on siteRuns on your own server, on CPU. No face recognition needed.

Good to know

People need to be at least 80 pixels tall in the picture. Colours are lost at night on infrared cameras. Histor is an extra pair of eyes, not a medical alarm: we test person-down on staged falls at your site, and agree the false-alarm level with you, before it goes live.
